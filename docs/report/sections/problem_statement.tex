\subsection{The Communication Barrier}

People who communicate in Pakistan Sign Language (PSL) and people who do not know it often have no shared language. In everyday settings such as a classroom, a shop, a clinic or a family gathering, a conversation between a signer and a non-signer usually depends on a human interpreter, on writing things down, or on improvised gestures. Interpreters are not always available. Writing is slow and depends on both people sharing a written language. Gestures break down as soon as the message becomes specific, for example a person's name or an address.

The burden of bridging this gap often falls on the signer. Non-signers who want to meet them halfway, such as relatives, classmates and teachers, need a way to learn and practise signs, and to check whether they are reading or producing a sign correctly.

\subsection{Why Start with Letters}

Fingerspelling, signing a word letter by letter, is commonly used for names, places and words that do not have an established sign (source needed; see the box below). A tool that recognises individual letters therefore helps exactly in the situations where improvised gestures fail.

Letters are also a sensible first step for a technical reason. Each static letter is a single hand shape that can be captured in one image, the set of classes is small and fixed, and every prediction can be checked against a known answer. Recognising words, sentences or signs that involve movement needs sequences of frames and a much larger dataset, which is outside what one course project can build and evaluate properly.

\subsection{Scope}

This project covers \textbf{static letter signs only}. The system receives a single image of one hand and returns the most likely letters with their confidence. It does not recognise motion-based signs, words or sentences, and it does not translate speech or text into signs.
